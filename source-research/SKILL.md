# Source Research

## How to read this skill

Always in force: `Primary first`, `Legal access only` and `Opened versus
seen` — every source you record is judged by these three. Conditional:
`Recording a source` — you add a row to a registry or a field to a record;
`Paywalls and gaps` — the primary source cannot be opened;
`Re-verification` — a field was first filled from a snippet or a secondary
source.

## Primary first

- The primary source is the document where the claim was first made: the
  original article, the official specification, the publisher's or author's
  page, the dataset itself.
- A review, a blog post, an encyclopedia entry or a search snippet is a route
  to the primary source, not a substitute for it.
- When only a secondary source is reachable, record it as secondary and say
  which primary source it reports.

## Legal access only

Use the publisher, the author's own pages, institutional repositories, open
access archives, official registries and libraries the user has access to.
Never use shadow libraries, pirated mirrors, or paywall-bypass tools, and do
not cite text obtained that way even if it would settle the question.

## Opened versus seen

Keep two states apart for every URL:

- **opened** — you loaded the page or document and read the relevant part;
- **seen** — it appeared in search results or a snippet and was not opened.

Only an opened source supports a field marked as verified
([evidence-citing](../evidence-citing/SKILL.md) code `V`). A snippet can tell
you where to look; it cannot be the evidence.

## Recording a source

For every source you use, record:

- the exact URL opened (not a search-results URL), and the date;
- title, authors, year, publisher or venue, identifier (DOI, ISBN, standard
  number) when one exists;
- access: open, paid, library, or permission-only;
- what you took from it, and where in it (page, section);
- the verification code.

## Paywalls and gaps

When the primary source is paywalled and no legal free copy exists, take the
figure from a secondary source only if you mark it as such, name the
secondary source, and list the unchecked point under the record's open
questions. Search for a legal free version (author preprint, institutional
copy, publisher sample) before settling for a secondary report.

## Re-verification

A field first filled from a snippet or a secondary source is provisional.
Re-verify it against the primary page before it is used in a decision, and
record the outcome either way. If the primary page disagrees, the primary
page wins and the discrepancy is noted.
